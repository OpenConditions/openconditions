# PUN inventory qualification

The PUN adapter implements the factual GSE/MASE inventory distributed by Cruscotto Italia (AgID). The catalogue and offline atlas keep it disabled pending review of CRS interpretation, full-generation runtime and egress-IP coordination. Existing CC BY attribution and the factual-product rights scope remain unchanged. Implementation and fixture tests do not authorize activation.

Ordinary `feeds:check` respects the disabled entry before any provider call. It also excludes enabled PUN entries with a configuration skip: this expensive operation needs a separate qualification run. A skipped source is not assessed and cannot establish recovery in monitoring evidence.

After a reviewer has accepted CRS interpretation and authorized a full run, an operator with exclusive ownership of the provider-facing egress IP can explicitly invoke `pnpm exec tsx scripts/qualify-pun.ts --full-generation --reviewed-crs-runtime --exclusive-egress-ip-single-worker`. This command acquires every shard in the frozen PUN manifest, verifies sizes and digests, rechecks the generation and parses the complete bundle. It neither publishes, accepts a cache nor enables the catalogue. It offers no sample-as-complete mode. A successful run is evidence for review, not automatic qualification or national census completeness.

Acquisition allows at most 10,000 shards, 30,006 actual requests including retries, four hours total elapsed time and 384 MiB of transferred response bodies including retries. The four-hour deadline starts before entering the shared host queue and covers rate waits, retries, requests and body consumption. The normal 10,002-request maximum pass needs about 167 minutes just for request spacing, leaving about 73 minutes for transfer and backoff. Manifest bodies are capped at 16 MiB, each shard at 8 MiB and listed shard bytes at 96 MiB. These are payload bounds, not peak-memory measurements.

A long Retry-After fails the active poll if it cannot fit its remaining deadline. The process retains the host's next-allowed timestamp, and later polls make zero requests while that timestamp lies beyond their budget. Cancellation and scheduler shutdown interrupt queue, pacing and body waits. Failed acquisitions never accept staged validators or replace last-good inventory. Backoff is process state; operators must preserve provider cooldown across a process restart rather than use a restart to evade it.

Runtime acquisition requires both `PUN_REVIEWED_CRS_RUNTIME=qualified` and `PUN_EGRESS_OWNERSHIP=exclusive-ip-single-worker`. The scheduler and fetch boundary enforce these exact assertions; setting only one does not opt in. The ownership assertion means one acquiring process across all hosts behind that public IP, with no other traffic to this provider from that IP. The process-wide queue enforces one connection and one request start per second. It cannot discover other machines behind NAT: operators unable to guarantee exclusive ownership must leave this assertion unset and the source disabled. Removing `disabled` is a separate reviewed catalogue change after qualification; these settings do not remove it.
