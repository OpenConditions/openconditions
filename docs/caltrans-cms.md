# Caltrans changeable message signs

The twelve district entries read only the agency-owned [CMS product](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/changeable-message-signs.html). Cameras, travel-time files, inferred closures and inferred equipment faults are outside this adapter. Each feed configures its district independently; the wire district and repeated indexes cannot identify signs reliably. Provider acquisition remains in the ingest framework. District 2 uses five-minute polling; the other districts use one minute. No numeric request quota is asserted.

A feature identity hashes the configured district, complete trimmed operational location name, county, route/suffix, direction, postmile/prefix and alignment using SHA-256. It does not depend on array order, index uniqueness, coordinates or current message. Coordinate and index corrections preserve identity. A name or road-position correction creates a new identity: automatic reconciliation would risk merging different devices. Repeated full identity tuples reject the whole payload, preserving last-good publication. Case, punctuation and abbreviations are significant; no unverified aliases are merged. District 7 repeats names across roads/directions, so name alone is insufficient. Long-term continuity remains unqualified. Positions outside a conservative California envelope are excluded, including placeholders and the Sacramento longitude typo; the adapter never repairs coordinates by guessing.

Each sign has one sign face and a structured display. Blank, explicitly out-of-service and unknown reporting states carry no text. Normal/flashing and extended two-page messages preserve page and line positions; a blank line is represented by a single space because the model requires nonempty localized text. The allowlisted `caltransCms` extras retain source index, configured district, raw record/message clocks, validated instants, phase fonts and phase duration. No other payload fields enter extras. Observation phenomenon time is the retrieval observation, not an assertion that the message changed then. Record modification and message change remain separate extras. Strict Los Angeles conversion accepts only one matching instant and rejects invalid dates, 1969/1970 sentinels, DST gaps and ambiguous folds.

The twelve catalogue entries are enabled after bounded official-document and two-generation live-payload qualification on November 9, 2026. They operate as partial snapshots: `caltransSnapshotQualified` stays unset because the provider supplies no deletion protocol, total count or authoritative empty-snapshot marker. An absent sign receives no renewed expiry. Each present feature and reading expires fifteen minutes after its own successful verification, even while other signs continue updating. This keeps old message-change dates separate from current display verification. An empty, failed, ambiguous or unreadable unqualified payload preserves last-good publication without extending it. The explicit snapshot qualification control remains available only for independently established completeness authority; local synthetic complete-empty tests exercise that control without asserting provider deletion semantics. District failures remain independent.

The committed activation fixtures contain two bounded captures of all twelve district endpoints, separated by at least five minutes, with acquisition metadata and hashes. Tests replay those bytes through the catalogue, production registry, worker, disposable PostGIS and public API. This establishes local operation with actual payloads, not sustained provider uptime, a physical device census or deployment acceptance.

The [field contract](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/documentation/cms/cms-field-description.html), [layout](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/documentation/cms/cms-json-layout-example.html) and [reporting matrix](https://cwwp2.dot.ca.gov/documentation/cms/cms-district-reporting-matrix.html) describe source semantics. Preserve third-party ownership exceptions, public-egress restrictions and Caltrans credit. Local captured-fixture and disposable PostGIS/API tests establish source behavior only; they do not establish deployment acceptance or provider uptime.
